A [[Sets|Set]] is countable if either it is finite or it can be made in one to one correspondence with the set of natural numbers. A countable set that is not finite is said to countable infinite

## Definition
A set $S$ is countable if:
- Its cardinality $\text{ | } S \text{ | }$ is less than or equal to $\aleph_0$, the cardinality of the set of natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$.
- There exists an [[Injective functions|Injective function]] from $S$ to $\mathbb{N}$.
- $S$ is empty or there exists a [[Surjective functions|Surjective function]] from $\mathbb{N}$ to $S$.
- There exists a [[Bijective functions|Bijective]] mapping between $S$ and a subset of $\mathbb{N}$.
- $S$ is either finite $( \text{ | } S \text{ | } < \aleph_0 )$ or countably infinite.
