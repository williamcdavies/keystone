The null space of a linear map is the part of the domain which is mapped to the zero vector of the co-domain. The null space is always a linear subspace of the domain. That is, given a linear map $L : V \rightarrow W$ between two [[Linear spaces]] $V$ and $W$, the null space of $L$ is the linear space of all elements $v$ of $V$ such that $L(v) = 0$, where $0$ denotes the zero element in $W$, or more symbolically:
$$\ker(L) = \{ v \in V \mid L(v) = 0 \} = L^{-1}(0)$$
