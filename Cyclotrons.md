A cyclotron is a type of particle accelerator used to accelerate charge particles outward from the centre of a flat cylindrical vacuum chamber along a spiral path. The particles are held to a spiral trajectory by a static [[Magnetic fields|Magnetic field]] and accelerated by a rapidly varying [[Electric fields|Electric field]].

## Particle Energy
Each time a particle crosses the accelerating gap in a cyclotron, it is given an accelerating force by the electric field across the gap, and the total particle energy gain can be calculated by multiplying the increase per crossing by the number of times the particle crosses the gap.

However, given the typically high number of revolutions, it is usually simpler to estimate the energy by combing the equation for frequency in circular motion:
$$f = \frac{v}{2 \pi r}$$

with the cyclotron frequency equation to yield:
$$v = \frac{qBr}{m}$$

The kinetic energy for particles with speed $v$ is therefore given by:
$$E = \frac{1}{2} mv^2 = \frac{q^2B^2r^2}{2m}$$
