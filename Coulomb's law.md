Coulomb's inverse-square law is an experimental law of physics that provides a definition for the conservative electrostatic force (Coulomb force) between two electrically charged particles at rest.

Coulomb's law states that the magnitude of the attractive or repulsive electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the magnitudes of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them:
$$\text{ | } F \text{ | } = k_e \frac{ \text{ | } q_1 \text{ | } \text{ | } q_2 \text{ | } }{r^2}$$

where $k_e = \frac{1}{4 \pi \epsilon_0} = 8.99 \times 10^9 N \cdot m^2 \cdot C^{-2}$ is the [[Coulomb constant]].

Coulomb's law can be written in vector form as
$$\vec{F}_{12} = k_e \frac{q_1q_2}{r^2} \widehat{r}_{12}$$

where $\widehat{r}_{12}$ is a unit vector directed from $q1$ toward $q_2$.
