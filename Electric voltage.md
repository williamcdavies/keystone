The electric voltage between location $a$ and location $b$ is the ratio of $dw$ to $dq$, where $dw$ is the energy in joules required to move positive charge $dq$ from $a$ to $b$ (or negative charge form $b$ to $a$).
$$v_{ab} = \frac{dw}{dq} \qquad 1V = 1 \frac{J}{C}$$

If an [[Electric current]] enters a circuit element at its positive terminal and exits at its negative terminal, the electric voltage and electric current adhere to the passive convention (voltage drop).

If an electric current enters a circuit element at its negative terminal and exits at its positive terminal, the electric voltage and electric current do not adhere to the passive convention (voltage rise).

## Electric Voltage Sources
When two or more electric voltage sources are placed in series, they can be replaced by a single equivalent voltage source whose voltage is equivalent to the sum of the individual voltages. Electric voltage sources cannot be placed in parallel.
