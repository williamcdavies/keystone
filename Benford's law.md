Benford's law is an observation that in many real-life sets of numerical data, the leading digit is likely to be small. In sets that obey the law, the number $1$ appears as the leading significant digit about $30\%$ of the time, while $9$ appears as the leading significant digit less than $5\%$ of the time. Uniformly distributed digits would each occur about $11.1\%$ of the time. Benford's law also makes predictions about the distribution of second digits, third digits, digit combinations, and so on.
