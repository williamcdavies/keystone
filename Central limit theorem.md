The central limit theorem states that if a sufficiently large number of samples are taken from a population, the samples' mean distribution will be normal, irrespective of the population distribution.

The central limit theorem states that the [[Sample mean]] of the samples' mean distribution will be equivalent to the [[Population mean]]: $\overline{x} = \mu$ and that the [[Sample variance]] of the samples' mean distribution will be equivalent to the [[Population variance]] divided by the the sample size: $s^2 = \frac{ \sigma^2 }{n}$.
