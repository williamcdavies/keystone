In a Carnot cycle, a system or engine transfers energy in the form of [[Heat]] between two thermal reservoirs at temperatures $T_H$ and $T_C$, and a part of this transferred energy is converted to the [[Work]] don by the system. The cycle is reversible, and [[Entropy (classical thermodynamics)]] is conserved, merely transferred between the thermal reservoirs and the system without any gain or loss. When work is applied to the system, heat moves from the cold to hot reservoir. When heat moves from the hot to the cold reservoir, the system applies work to the environment. The work $W$ done by the system or engine to the environment per Carnot cycle depends on the temperatures of the thermal reservoirs and the entropy transferred form the hot reservoir to the system $\Delta S$ per cycle such as $W = (T_H - T_C) \Delta S = (T_H - T_C) \frac{Q_H}{T_H}$, where $Q_H$ is heat transferred from the hot reservoir to the system per cycle.

The efficiency $\eta$ is defines to be:
$$\eta = \frac{W}{Q_H} = \frac{Q_H - Q_C}{Q_H} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$$

where
- $W$ is the work done by the system (energy exiting the system as work),
- $Q_C < 0$ is the heat taken from the system (heat energy leaving the system),
- $Q_H > 0$ is the heat put into the system (heat energy leaving the system),
- $T_C$ is the absolute [[Temperature]] of the cold reservoir, and
- $T_H$ is the absolute temperature of the hot reservoir.
