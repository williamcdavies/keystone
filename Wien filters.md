A Wien filter also known as a velocity selector is a device consisting of perpendicular electric and magnetic fields that can be used as a velocity filter for charged particles.

Any charged particle in an [[Electric fields|Electric field]] will feel a force proportional to the charge and the field strength such that $\vec{F} = q \vec{E}$, where $F$ is force, $q$ is charge, and $E$ is electric field strength. Similarly, any particle moving in a [[Magnetic fields|Magnetic field]] will feel a force proportional to the velocity and charge of the particle. The force felt by any particle is then equal to $\vec{F} = q \vec{v} \times \vec{B}$, where $F$ is force, $q$ is the charge on the particle, $v$ is the velocity of the particle, and $B$ is the strength of the magnetic field. In the case of a velocity selector, the magnetic field is always at a $90 \degree$ angle to the velocity and the force is simplified to $F = qvB$ in the direction described by the cross product.

Setting the two force to equal magnitude in opposite directions it can be shown that $v = \frac{E}{B}$.

Which means that any combination of electric $\vec{E}$ and magnetic  $\vec{B}$ fields will allow charged particles with only velocity $\vec{v}$ through.
