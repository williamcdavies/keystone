Thermodynamics is the study of [[Heat]] and [[Temperature]] and their relation to energy, [[Entropy (classical thermodynamics)]], and the physical properties of matter and radiation. The behaviour of thermodynamic systems is determined by four natural laws. These laws are as follows:
- $0$th law: If two systems are each in [[Thermal equilibrium]] with a third system, then they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.
- $1$st law: When energy passes into or out of a system, the system's [[Internal energy]] changes in accordance with the law of [[Conservation of energy]]. The $1$st law of thermodynamics can be expressed: $\Delta K + \Delta V + \Delta U = W + Q + T_{ \text{ mw } } + T_{ \text{ mt } } + T_{ \text{ et } } + T_{ \text{ er } }$.
- $2$nd law: In a natural thermodynamic process, the sum of the entropies of the interacting thermodynamic systems never decreases (heat does not spontaneously pass from a colder body to a warmer body).
- $3$rd law: A system's entropy approaches a constant value as the temperature approaches absolute zero.
