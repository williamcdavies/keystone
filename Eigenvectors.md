An eigenvector is a vector that has its direction unchanged by a given linear transformation. More precisely, an eigenvector, $\vec{v}$, of a linear transformation, $T$, is scaled by a constant factor, $\lambda$, when the linear transformation is applied to it: $T \vec{v} = \lambda \vec{v}$. The corresponding eigenvalue is the multiplying factor $\lambda$.

Consider an $n \times n$ matrix $A$ and a nonzero vector $\vec{v}$ of length $n$. If multiplying $A$ with $\vec{v}$ simply scaled $\vec{v}$ by a factor of $\lambda$, where $\lambda$ is a scalar, then $\vec{v}$ is called an eigenvector of $A$, and $\lambda$ is the corresponding eigenvalue. This relationship can be expressed as $A \vec{v} = \lambda \vec{v}$.
