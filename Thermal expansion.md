As the [[Temperature]] of particles increase, they experience an increased rate of oscillation. This oscillation weakens the intermolecular forces between particles resulting in an expansion of the substance.

## Thermal Expansion in One Dimension
The dependence of thermal expansion on temperature, substance, and original length is modelled in the equation 
$$\frac{dL}{dT} = \alpha L$$ 

where $L$ is the original length $\frac{dL}{dT}$ is the change in length with respect to temperature, and $\alpha$ is the coefficient of linear expansion, a material property that varies slightly with temperature. As $\alpha$ is nearly constant and also very small, for practical purposes we use the linear approximation:
$$\Delta L \approx \alpha L \Delta T$$

## Thermal Expansion in Two Dimensions
For small temperature changes, the change in area $\Delta A$ is given by
$$\Delta A = 2 \alpha A \Delta T$$

where $\Delta A$ is the range area $A$, $\Delta T$ is the change in temperature and $\alpha$ is the coefficient of linear expansion, which varies slightly with temperature. 

## Thermal Expansion in Three Dimensions
The relationship between volume and temperature $\frac{dV}{dT}$ is given by $\frac{dV}{dT} = \beta V \Delta T$ where $\beta$ is the coefficient of volume expansion. As you can show in exercise, $\beta = 3 \alpha$. This equation is usually written as 
$$\Delta V = \beta V \Delta T$$
