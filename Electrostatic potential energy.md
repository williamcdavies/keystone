Electrostatic potential energy is a potential energy that results from conservative Coulomb forces and is associated with the configuration of a particular set of point charges within a defined system.

The electrostatic potential energy, $U_E$, of one point charge $q$ at position $\vec{r}$ in the presence of an electric field $\vec{E}$ is defined as the negative of the [[Work]] $W$ done by the electrostatic force to bring it from the reference position $\vec{r_{ \text{ ref } }}$ to position $\vec{r}$
$$U_E( \vec{r} ) = -W_{ \vec{r_{ \text{ ref } }} } \rightarrow \vec{r} = -\int_{ \vec{r_{ \text{ ref } }} }^{ \vec{r} } {q \vec{E} ( \vec{r} ') \cdot d \vec{r} '}$$

The electrostatic potential energy, $U_E$, of one point charge $q$ in the presence of an electric potential $V$ is defined as the product of the charge and the electric potential
$$U_E = qV$$
