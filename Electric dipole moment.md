The electric dipole moment is a measure of the separation of positive and negative electrical charges within a system.

The electric dipole moment of two point charges can be expressed in vector form
$$\vec{p} = q \vec{d}$$

where $\vec{d}$ is the displacement vector pointing from the negative charge to the positive charge.

## Energy & Torque
An object with an electric dipole moment $\vec{p}$ is subject to a [[Torque]] $\tau$ when placed in an external [[Electric fields|Electric field]] $\vec{E}$. The torque tends to align the dipole with the field. A dipole aligned parallel to an electric field has lower potential energy than a dipole making some non-zero angle with it. For a spatially uniform electric field across the small region occupied by the dipole, the energy $U$ and the torque $\vec{ \tau }$ are given by
$$U = -\vec{p} \cdot \vec{E}$$
$$\vec{ \tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$$

The scalar [[Dot products|Dot product]] and the negative sign shows the potential energy minimises when the dipole is parallel with the field, maximises when antiparallel, and is zero when perpendicular.
