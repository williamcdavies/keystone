Gauss's law states that the [[Electric flux]] of the [[Electric fields|Electric field]] out of an arbitrary closed surface is proportional to the electric charge enclosed by the surface, irrespective of how that charge is distributed. Therefore, the net flux through any closed surface surrounding a point charge $q$ can be given by $\frac{q}{ \epsilon_0 }$.

Gauss's law may be expressed as
$$\Phi_E = \frac{Q}{ \epsilon_0 }$$

where $\Phi_E$ is the electric flux through a closed surface $S$ enclosing any volume $V$, $Q$ is the total charge enclosed within $V$, and $\epsilon_0$ is the [[Permittivity of free space]].
