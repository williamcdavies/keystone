In the cylindrical coordinate system, a point in space is represented by the ordered triple $(r, \theta, z)$, where $(r, \theta)$ are the polar coordinates of the point's projection in the $xy$-plane and $z$ is the usual $z$-coordinate in the cartesian coordinate system.

## Conversion Between Cylindrical & Cartesian Coordinates
The rectangular coordinates $(x, y, z)$ and the cylindrical coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$ of a point are related as follows:

These equations are used to convert from cylindrical coordinates to rectangular coordinates.
- $x = r \cos{ \theta }$
- $y = r \sin{ \theta }$
- $z = z$

These equations are used to convert from rectangular coordinates to cylindrical coordinates
- $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$
- $\tan{ \theta } = \frac{y}{x}$
- $z = z$
