Logical operators are used to combine [[Propositions]]. These logical operators are identical to logical operators used for programming.

- $\neg$ denotes negation.
- $\land$ denotes conjunction.
- $\lor$ denotes disjunction.
- $\oplus$ denotes exclusive or.
- $\rightarrow$ denotes condition.
- $\leftrightarrow$ denotes bicondition.

## References
Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications Sec. 1.1.2 - Kenneth H. Rosen
> "Let $p$ be a proposition. The *negation* of $p$, denoted by $\neg p$ (also denoted by $\overline{p}$) is the statement - "It is not the case that $p$." - The proposition $\neg p$ is read "not $p$." The truth value of the negation of $p$, $\neg p$, is the opposition of the truth value of $p$."

> "Let $p$ and $q$ be propositions. The *conjunction* of $p$ and $q$, denoted by $p \land q$, is the proposition "$p$ and $q$". The conjunction$p \land q$ is true when both $p$ and $q$ are true and is false otherwise."

> "Let $p$ and $q$ be propositions. The *disjunction* of $p$ and $q$, denoted by $p \lor q$, is the proposition "$p$ or $q$". The conjunction$p \lor q$ is false when both $p$ and $q$ are false and is true otherwise."

> "Let $p$ and $q$ be propositions. The *exclusive or* of $p$ and $q$, denoted by $p \oplus q$, is the proposition "$p$ XOR $q$". The conjunction$p \oplus q$ is true when exactly one of $p$ and $q$ is true and is false otherwise."

Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications Sec. 1.1.3 - Kenneth H. Rosen
> "Let $p$ and $q$ be propositions. The *conditional statement* $p \rightarrow q$ is the proposition "if $p$, then $q$." The conditional statement $p \rightarrow q$ is false when $p$ is true and $q$ is false, and true otherwise. In the conditional statement $p \rightarrow q$, $p$ is called the the *hypothesis* (or *antecedent* or *premise*) and $q$ is called the *conclusion* (or *consequence*)."

> "Let $p$ and $q$ be propositions. The *biconditional statement* $p \leftrightarrow q$ is the proposition "$p$ if and only if $q$." The biconditional statement $p \leftrightarrow q$ is true when $p$ and $q$ have the same truth values, and is false otherwise. Biconditional statements are also called *bi-implications*."
