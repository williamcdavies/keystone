An isobaric process is a type of thermodynamic process in which the [[Pressure]] $P$ of a system remains constant.

The work $W$ done by an ideal gas undergoing an isothermal process can be modelled by $W = P \Delta V$, where $P$ is the pressure and $\Delta V$ is the change in volume.

The change in [[Internal energy]] for an ideal gas undergoing an isobaric process can be modelled by $\Delta U = nC_v \Delta T$, where $n$ is the number of moles, $C_v$ is the constant-volume [[Specific heat]], and $\Delta T$ is the change in [[Temperature]].
