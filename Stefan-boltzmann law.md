The Stefan-Boltzmann law describes the intensity of the thermal radiation emitted by matter in terms of that matter's [[Temperature]].

For an ideal absorber/emitter or black body, the Stefan-Boltzmann law states that the total energy radiated per unit surface area per unit time is directly proportional to the fourth power of the black body's temperature, $T$.
$$M \degree = \sigma T^4$$

The constant of proportionality, $\omega$, is called the Stefan-Boltzmann constant. It has the value
$$\sigma = 5.670374419 \cdot 10^-8 \space W \cdot m^{-2} \cdot K^{-4}$$

To find the total power, $P$, radiated from an object, multiply the radiant existence by the object's surface area, $A$:
$$P = A \cdot M = A \epsilon \sigma T^4$$
