The electrical resistance of an object is a measure of its opposition to the flow of [[Electric current]]. Its reciprocal quantity is electrical conductance, measuring the ease with which an electric current passes. Electrical resistance shares some conceptual parallels with mechanical [[Friction]].

The electrical resistance $R$ of an object is defined as the ratio of [[Electric voltage]] $V$ across it to electric current $I$ through it, while the [[Electrical conductance]] $G$ is the reciprocal:
$$R = \frac{V}{I} \qquad G = \frac{I}{V} = \frac{1}{R}$$

## Relation to Resistivity & Conductivity
The electrical resistance $R$ and electrical conductance $G$ of a conductor of uniform cross section can be computed as
$$R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$$
$$G = \sigma \frac{A}{l}$$

where $l$ is the length of the conductor, $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the conductor, $\sigma$ is the [[Electrical conductivity]], and $\rho$ is the [[Electrical resistivity]].

## Temperature Dependence
The electrical resistivity of most materials changes with [[Temperature]]. If the temperature $T$ does not very too much, a linear approximation is typically used:
$$\rho (T) = \rho_0 [1 + \alpha (T - T_0)]$$

where $\alpha$ is called the temperature coefficient of resistivity, $T_0$ is a fixed reference temperature, and $\rho_0$ is the receptivity at temperature $T_0$.
