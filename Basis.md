A basis $B$ of a vector space $V$ over a field $F$ (such as the real numbers $\mathbb{R}$) is a linearly independent [[Subsets|Subset]] of $V$ that spans $V$. This means that a subset $B$ of $V$ is a basis if it satisfies the two following conditions:
1. for every finite subset $\{ v_1, v_2, \dots , v_m \}$ of $B$, if $c_1v_1 + \dots + c_mv_m = 0$ for some $c_1, \dots , c_m$ in $F$, then $c_1 = c_m = 0$
2. for every vector $v$ in $V$, one can choose $a_1, \dots , a_n$ in $F$ and $v_1, \dots , v_n$ in $B$ such that $v = a_1v_1 + \dots + a_nv_n$
