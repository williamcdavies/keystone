An electric current is the flow of charged particles, such as electrons or ions, moving through an electrical conductor or space. It is defined as the net rate of flow of electric charge through a surface. The moving particles are called charge carriers, which may be one of several types of particles, depending on the conductor.

The electric current $I$ in a conductor is defined as
$$I \equiv \frac{dQ}{dt}$$

where $dQ$ is the charge that passes through a cross section of the conduction in a time interval $dt$.

The average electric current in a conductor is related to the motion of the charge carriers through the relationship
$$I_{ \text{ avg } } = nqv_dA$$

where $n$ is the density of charge carriers, $q$ is the charge on each carrier, $v_d$ is the [[Drift velocity]], and $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the conductor.

Electric current obtained by the equation:
$$q(t) = \int_{ -\infty }^t{i \space d \tau } = \int_0^t{i \space d \tau } + q(0)$$

## Electric Current Sources
When two or more electric current sources are placed in parallel, they can be replaced by a single equivalent current source whose current is equivalent to the sum of the individual currents. Electric current sources cannot be placed in series.
