Internal resistance $r$ of any real electrical source is the [[Electrical resistance]] that charge moving through the material of the source encounters.

The presence of internal resistance in a source of [[Electromotive forces|Electromotive force]] means that the potential difference across the terminals of the electrical source is always less than its electromotive force.

By the [[Conservation of energy]], energy delivered by an electromotive force is equivalent to the sum of the energy dissipated by external resistance and the energy dissipated by internal resistance:
$$\begin{align}
I_{ \epsilon } &= IV_{AB} + IV_r \\\\
\epsilon &= V_{AB} + V_r \\\\
\epsilon &= IR + Ir \\\\
IR &= \epsilon - Ir \\\\
V_{AB} &= \epsilon - Ir
\end{align}$$
