Superconductivity is a set of physical properties observed in superconductors: materials where [[Electrical resistance]] vanishes and [[Magnetic fields]] are expelled from the material. Unlike an ordinary metallic conductor, whose resistance decreases gradually as its temperature is lowered, a superconductor has a characteristic critical temperature below which the resistance drops abruptly to zero. An [[Electric current]] through a loop of superconducting wire can persist indefinitely with no source of [[Electric power]].
